# Citation and evidence policy

## Minimum citation

For each material legal proposition, identify:

- instrument title and number;
- issuing authority;
- exact article, clause, and point when available;
- publication or effective-date detail relevant to the question;
- direct official source URL; and
- the date on which current status was checked when relevant.

A citation must point to text the agent actually inspected. Do not generate article numbers, quotations, source titles, or URLs from memory. If the source page lacks stable anchors, cite the official document page and give the exact provision in text.

## Quote versus paraphrase

Use quotation marks only for wording copied from the inspected source, preserve its meaning, and keep quotations limited to the relevant phrase. Mark summaries as paraphrases. Do not combine separate provisions inside one quotation or silently modernize wording.

## Claim-to-source mapping

- Attach a citation to the specific claim it supports.
- Explain when a cited provision supplies only part of the rule or depends on another instrument.
- Do not cite a broad source page as if it proves a specific effective date unless that page states it.
- Separate a user's account, an assumption, a retrieved source fact, and the agent's explanation.
- If there is no supporting source, label the proposition unverified or omit it; never fabricate a citation to make the answer look complete.

## Compact answer form

`[Plain-language proposition] (instrument number, issuer, Article X, Clause Y; effective/status checked as of YYYY-MM-DD; official URL).`

Use a separate citation for a distinct material proposition if its legal basis differs.
