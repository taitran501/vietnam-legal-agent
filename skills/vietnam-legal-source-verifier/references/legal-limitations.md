# Legal limitations and safe stops

This skill produces preliminary legal research, not a legal opinion or a prediction of a court, agency, employer, or other decision-maker. Make that boundary clear when applying law to an individual's facts, deadlines, rights, penalties, or other consequential decisions.

## Keep facts and law separate

- Treat statements, documents, screenshots, and dates supplied by the user as user-provided unless independently verified.
- State any assumption explicitly. Do not turn an allegation into a finding.
- Ask a concise follow-up if an unknown date, location, party status, document, or procedural stage changes which law applies.

## Stop or narrow the answer when

- the primary source cannot be located or inspected;
- the exact text, current version, amendment, or commencement date is uncertain, including when scanned text is unreadable or OCR checks disagree;
- official materials conflict and the conflict affects the result;
- the user's facts are too incomplete for a meaningful application; or
- the user requests a guaranteed outcome, evasion, or a definitive individualized opinion unsupported by the available record.

A safe stop should be useful: say what was verified, what could not be verified, why that gap matters, and which official document or factual detail would allow the research to continue. When appropriate, suggest timely review by a qualified Vietnamese legal professional, especially for active deadlines or consequential decisions.
